What counts as personal data at AcmeCorp

Per our legal review in September 2023, the following are classified as personal data under GDPR Article 4:

  • Full name + email (any combination)
  • IP addresses (yes, even anonymized ranges)
  • Payment tokens linked to a user ID
  • Browser fingerprints stored longer than 24h
  • Device IDs linked to user accounts

How to handle a GDPR deletion request

This process was updated on 2024-01-22 after the incident with the analytics pipeline (see post-mortem in LEGAL-198). Steps:

  1. User submits deletion request via privacy@acmecorp.com
  2. James Okafor logs it in the GDPR tracker spreadsheet within 24h
  3. Engineering receives a Jira ticket (auto-created by James) with affected user_id
  4. Engineering must purge: primary DB, analytics warehouse (Snowflake), email logs (Mailchimp), and payment tokens (Stripe/NovaPay)
  5. Confirmation email sent to user within 30 days

Critical edge case: If the user has an active subscription, do NOT delete until subscription end date. This was clarified after a legal dispute in Q3 2023.

Data retention rules

  • User activity logs: 90 days max
  • Payment records: 7 years (tax law requirement — confirmed by auditor)
  • Support tickets: 3 years
  • Marketing emails: delete on unsubscribe within 48h

History of key decisions

The 90-day activity log limit was debated heavily in October 2023. The Data team (led by Priya Nair) wanted 1 year for ML model training. Legal insisted on 90 days. The compromise: anonymized aggregate stats can be kept indefinitely, but individual-linked logs cannot. This decision was made in a meeting on 2023-10-15 (transcript available).
